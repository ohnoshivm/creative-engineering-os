# Phase 6: The 3-Gate Scaling Ladder + Retention System

Operators who try to jump from $5K to $50K in one move break around $12K–$15K/month.
They didn't build the audit log and retention motion at Gate 1, so the book leaks faster
than they can fill it. They spend 6 months climbing back to $20K with damaged trust on
half their accounts.

**The gates are sequential. You don't skip them.**

---

## GATE 1: $5K MRR — "Make It Stand Up"

### What Breaks First
Your own delivery time. Every new client adds proportional hours. You're doing 60-hour
weeks and the math doesn't work.

### The Fix
- Wire 2–3 agent workflows for the highest-frequency tasks
- Claude Code drafting + memory layer for client context + Roman for unattended runs
- Set a $4K–$5K/month productized floor with public pricing (buyer chooses themselves)
- **Do NOT hire a contractor at $5K MRR. Hire agents.**

### Hiring Rule at Gate 1
Bring on a contractor ONLY if the highest-frequency task is something an agent cannot
reliably do yet. Otherwise wait.

---

## GATE 2: $20K MRR — "Buy Back Your Time"

### What Breaks First
You. The bottleneck at $20K is rarely throughput — it's executive function.
You are the QA gate + salesperson + ops manager + renewal owner.
Sleep is gone. Churn risk is climbing.

### The Contrarian Fix
Hire a **project manager contractor** before a delivery contractor.
- 10–15 hours/week
- $30–50/hr US-based senior PM, $15–25/hr offshore
- Hand them: the calendar, client comms, QA-against-checklist

Add 3–4 more agent workflows:
- Outreach reply triage
- Memory-driven context loading
- Slack MCP renewal alerts

**The first contractor is for YOU, not for the work.**
The moment scheduling and comms stop routing through your brain, delivery throughput
rises automatically.

### Hiring Rule at Gate 2
The solo-to-hire threshold: $15K–$20K MRR sustained for 3 months before hiring a
full-time team member. Anything earlier and you haven't learned the workflow well
enough to hand it off.

---

## GATE 3: $50K MRR — "Productize the Operator"

### What Breaks First
You cannot be the central node. Every decision still routes through you and the system
cannot scale past your calendar.

### The Fix
- Move top 1–3 accounts onto **hybrid base + outcome pricing**:
  40–60% base retainer + remainder earned through measurable results
- Raise productized floor for new accounts to **$8K/month**
- Take contractor to 20–25 hrs/week or convert to full-time
- Run 8–12 agent workflows unattended
- **Move to value-based pricing or lose the next renewal cycle**

### Industry Context at Gate 3
Less than 15% of top-performing agencies are still billing hourly at $50K MRR.
The entire industry has moved to value-based pricing. You move with it or you don't
survive the renewal cycle.

---

## THE 3 ANCHORING NUMBERS

| Number | Trigger |
|--------|---------|
| **$3K–$5K MRR** | First contractor — ONLY if highest-frequency task can't be done by agents yet |
| **$15K–$20K MRR** (sustained 3 months) | First full-time hire |
| **$4K–$5K/month** | Minimum viable account price for solo operator P&L to clear |

Any account below the floor at Gate 1 gets nudged to the new floor at Gate 2.

---

## THE RETENTION SYSTEM (Build at Gate 1 or Never)

> You cannot bolt on retention at $20K MRR.
> You design for it from the first deliverable.

**Every client gets from day one:**
- Audit log (every run logged with inputs, outputs, model, reviewer)
- Weekly cadence reports (agent posts every Friday into shared Slack channel — pulled
  from client's HubSpot, Stripe, analytics — no human touch)
- Quarterly business review
- Per-client custom annual planning session

### The Renewal Math
- Operators who treat retention as a Phase 6 problem: break at $12K
- Operators who treat retention as a Phase 1 design constraint: clear $50K with a 6-week waitlist

### What the Renewal Conversation Looks Like at Month 12
It is not a conversation about price.
It is a conversation about what they would lose:
18 months of agent-curated context, audit logs, memory layer profile, and delivery
history that walks out the door if they leave.

They almost never leave.

---

## THE PORTABILITY PRINCIPLE

Architect the workspace so the channel structure is **portable**.
If Slack reprices the bot economics tomorrow, you don't lose the company — you lose the URL.
The channel structure ports to Mattermost in an afternoon.

This is not paranoia. It is the discipline of building on rented infrastructure.
